# SAMPLE 2: BEDROOM/BATH MISMATCH (Downward Risk)

---

**PROPERTY IN QUESTION VS COMPS**
Relevancy: HIGH

The PIQ is a 2-bedroom / 1-bath property, while higher-priced comps are predominantly 3-bedroom / 2-bath homes. This represents a fundamentally different buyer pool. The blended average includes sales that are not comparable to the PIQ and likely overstates its achievable value.

**MATRIX TABLE — ACTIVES**
Relevancy: MID

There are four active listings. Three are 3/2 layouts priced at or above the E-Value level. One is a 2/1 priced at $247,000 and sitting at 72 days on market. The 2/1 active represents the PIQ's true competition and suggests buyer resistance for this layout at higher price points.

**MATRIX TABLE — PENDINGS / BACKUPS**
Relevancy: LOW

There are two pendings, both are 3/2 layouts. Their pricing reflects demand for larger functional profiles and is not transferable to the PIQ's 2/1 configuration. These pendings do not validate upside for the PIQ.

**MATRIX TABLE — CLOSED SALES**
Relevancy: HIGH

There are eight closed sales. Six are 3/2 layouts averaging $289/sqft. Two are 2/1 layouts averaging $241/sqft. The blended average of $274/sqft is misleading because it combines two distinct buyer pools. The PIQ must anchor to the 2/1 cluster at $241/sqft, not the inflated blend.

**E-VALUE INTERPRETATION**
Outcome: CAUTION — AVERAGE OVERSTATED

The E-Value of $298,670 uses the blended average, which includes 3/2 sales the PIQ cannot replicate without structural expansion. The appropriate anchor for a 2/1 layout is approximately $241/sqft, implying a value closer to $263,000. The E-Value may be overstated by 12-14%.

**MARKET CEILING & PUSH LOGIC**
Relevancy: HIGH

The market ceiling for 2/1 properties is defined by the two matching closed sales and the stale 2/1 active, not by 3/2 premiums. Buyers shopping for 3/2 homes are not cross-shopping 2/1 properties at similar prices. The ceiling for PIQ is approximately $263,000.

**WHAT MUST BE VERIFIED**
• Confirm no feasible layout expansion (can the PIQ be converted to 3/2?)
• Get contractor assessment on bedroom/bath addition feasibility and cost
• Confirm buyer resistance to 2/1 layouts at higher prices via agent feedback
• Recalculate acquisition basis using 2/1 cluster anchor (~$241/sqft)

If layout expansion is not feasible, the deal must work at the 2/1 price level or it does not work.

**CONCLUSION**

The layout mismatch introduces real downward risk. The blended average masks a structural disadvantage that buyers will recognize immediately. The PIQ should anchor to the 2/1 cluster, and the baseline should be treated as a ceiling rather than a starting point.

**PROPERTY IN QUESTION VS COMPS**
Relevancy: LOW

The PIQ aligns structurally with the dominant sold profile. Bed/bath count, square footage, and lot size fall within the typical range. No functional mismatch exists that would affect buyer pool or pricing.

**MATRIX TABLE — ACTIVES**
Relevancy: LOW

**MATRIX TABLE — PENDINGS / BACKUPS**

**MATRIX TABLE — CLOSED SALES**

**E-VALUE INTERPRETATION**
Outcome: CAUTION — AVERAGE DISTORTED

The E-Value of $347,890 uses the inflated average. The high comp's premium drivers (oversized lot at 2.1× average plus income-producing ADU) do not exist in the PIQ and cannot be replicated through renovation. Anchoring to the median cluster (~$289/sqft) produces an implied value closer to $322,000. The E-Value may be overstated by 7-8%.

**MARKET CEILING & PUSH LOGIC**
Relevancy: HIGH

The true ceiling for properties like the PIQ is defined by the six-comp cluster, not the outlier. The $378/sqft sale achieved its premium through lot and ADU — permanent features the PIQ does not possess. Using that sale to justify PIQ pricing would be mathematically valid but practically wrong.

**WHAT MUST BE VERIFIED**
• Confirm unique features driving the high sale (lot size, ADU, view, recent addition)
• Confirm those features do NOT exist in the PIQ and cannot be added
• Recalculate acquisition basis using cluster anchor (~$289/sqft)
• Verify no other outlier contamination exists in the remaining dataset

If the outlier is confirmed as non-transferable, anchor to the cluster and treat the inflated average as noise.

**CONCLUSION**

The average is fake. One non-comparable sale with unique premium drivers is inflating the baseline beyond what the PIQ can achieve. The PIQ should anchor to the median cluster, and acquisition basis must be recalculated to reflect achievable value.

All three have been sitting for 58, 67, and 71 days respectively, with two having taken price reductions. The market is visibly rejecting this pricing tier.

**MATRIX TABLE — PENDINGS / BACKUPS**
Relevancy: MID

There is one pending at $298,000 — below the stale actives but consistent with the closed baseline. This confirms buyers are clearing inventory at baseline prices while superior properties at higher prices continue to sit.

**MATRIX TABLE — CLOSED SALES**

**E-VALUE INTERPRETATION**
Outcome: BASELINE — CEILING CAPPED

The E-Value of $319,500 falls within a price band where comparable actives are sitting unsold with price reductions. While the E-Value calculation is mathematically valid, market reality shows resistance at this level. Upside is capped by visible buyer rejection.

**MARKET CEILING & PUSH LOGIC**
Relevancy: HIGH

The ceiling is not defined by what sellers are asking or what formulas produce — it is defined by what buyers are currently refusing to pay. **CONCLUSION**

# SAMPLE 5: SIZE BREAKS MARKET CEILING

---

**PROPERTY IN QUESTION VS COMPS**
Relevancy: HIGH

The PIQ is materially larger than the comp average — 2,680 sqft versus 2,120 sqft. While bed/bath counts and lot size align with the set, the size differential creates a ceiling breach when average $/sqft is applied to PIQ's superior square footage.

**MATRIX TABLE — ACTIVES**
Relevancy: MID

There are three active listings. The largest at 2,450 sqft is priced at $412,000 and has been sitting at 82 days with one price reduction already taken. Larger homes at higher prices are encountering measurable resistance in this market.

**MATRIX TABLE — PENDINGS / BACKUPS**
Relevancy: LOW

There is one pending at 2,180 sqft priced at $367,000 — below the implied E-Value level for the PIQ. This pending does not validate pricing above the proven closed range for larger homes.

**MATRIX TABLE — CLOSED SALES**
Relevancy: HIGH

There are eight closed sales. The highest sold price was $389,000 on a 2,380 sqft home at $163/sqft. Applying the closed average of $167/sqft to the PIQ's 2,680 sqft produces $447,560 — exceeding the highest proven sale by $58,000. No buyer has paid at that level in this market.

**E-VALUE INTERPRETATION**
Outcome: CEILING BREACH — NO CLOSED PROOF

The E-Value of $447,560 exceeds the highest closed sale by approximately 15%. Size premiums are theoretical until a buyer actually pays them. The market has established a transaction ceiling at $389,000, and no closed evidence supports a value above it regardless of what the $/sqft math produces.

**MARKET CEILING & PUSH LOGIC**
Relevancy: HIGH

Additional square footage does not translate linearly to additional value in this market. The stale 2,450 sqft active at 82 DOM reinforces that larger properties face absorption challenges at premium prices. The highest closed defines the ceiling until a new sale breaks through it.

**WHAT MUST BE VERIFIED**
• Cap acquisition basis at the highest closed ($389,000) or apply a ceiling discount
• Monitor the pending closely — if it closes above current ceiling, the ceiling rises
• Confirm PIQ's square footage is accurate and fully permitted (check for unpermitted additions)
• Verify buyer demand profile — is there a size penalty in this market or simply a ceiling?

Do not underwrite to a value no buyer has proven they will pay.

**CONCLUSION**

The $/sqft math produces a value, but the market has not confirmed it. Size-adjusted E-Value exceeds the proven ceiling by a material margin. Cap the working value at what buyers have actually paid, not what the formula suggests is possible.

**MATRIX TABLE — PENDINGS / BACKUPS**
Relevancy: MID

There are two pendings. Both are standard-condition properties that went under contract in under 21 days. No premium-condition pendings currently exist — buyers in this market are demonstrating preference for value-priced inventory over top-tier finishes.

**MATRIX TABLE — CLOSED SALES**

There are nine closed sales. Premium-condition closeds (3 sales) averaged $318/sqft but required 78 DOM to find a buyer. Standard-condition closeds (6 sales) averaged $284/sqft but moved in only 32 DOM. The premium pricing tier exists, but it carries a significant and measurable time penalty.

**E-VALUE INTERPRETATION**
Outcome: BASELINE + STRATEGY WARNING

The E-Value of $347,200 is achievable if the PIQ is renovated to premium condition with high-end finishes. However, the data clearly shows premium-condition homes take 2.4× longer to sell in this market. **MARKET CEILING & PUSH LOGIC**
Relevancy: HIGH

The optimal execution strategy may be mid-tier renovation targeting $284/sqft with 30-day expected absorption rather than full flip targeting $318/sqft with 75+ day exposure and carrying cost drag.

**WHAT MUST BE VERIFIED**
• Calculate holding cost difference at actual capital cost across both timeline scenarios
• Define renovation scope that achieves "standard-condition" versus "premium-condition" presentation
• Confirm buyer profile in this market — first-time buyers (price-sensitive) or move-up buyers (quality-focused)?
• Model both strategies: would two faster turns at lower margin outperform one premium flip?

Over-improvement is not always the optimal strategy — even when higher $/sqft comps exist in the dataset.

**CONCLUSION**

Higher prices exist at premium condition, but velocity data strongly suggests this market prefers "good enough" over "perfect." The premium pricing comes with a time penalty that may fully erode the incremental margin. Run the numbers on both renovation strategies before committing to full-scope improvement.

# SAMPLE 9: NEW CONSTRUCTION CONTAMINATION

---

**PROPERTY IN QUESTION VS COMPS**
Relevancy: HIGH

The PIQ was built in 2008. The comp set average year built is 2017 because it includes six new construction sales from 2021-2024. New construction and 16-year-old resale are fundamentally different products competing for different buyer segments at materially different price points.

**MATRIX TABLE — ACTIVES**
Relevancy: MID

There are five active listings. Three are new construction priced between $338/sqft and $352/sqft. Two are resale homes priced at $282/sqft and $291/sqft and sitting longer than the new construction listings. The market clearly segments and prices by product type.

**MATRIX TABLE — PENDINGS / BACKUPS**
Relevancy: MID

There are three pendings. Two are new construction at $331/sqft and $339/sqft, both went pending quickly. One is resale at $276/sqft and took 44 days to go under contract. New construction has a measurable velocity and pricing advantage that resale cannot match.

**MATRIX TABLE — CLOSED SALES**
Relevancy: HIGH

There are eleven closed sales with a blended average of $312/sqft. However, new construction sales (6) averaged $341/sqft while resale pre-2015 builds (5) averaged $271/sqft. The blended average masks a 26% pricing gap between product types. The PIQ must anchor to resale comps, not the contaminated blend.

**E-VALUE INTERPRETATION**
Outcome: CAUTION — PRODUCT TYPE CONTAMINATION

The E-Value of $378,560 uses the blended average, which is materially inflated by new construction sales. New construction commands a structural premium the PIQ cannot achieve — builder warranty, modern floor plans, energy efficiency, and never-occupied status. The appropriate anchor for resale is ~$271/sqft, implying a value closer to $329,000. The E-Value may be overstated by 13-15%.

**MARKET CEILING & PUSH LOGIC**
Relevancy: HIGH

The ceiling for resale product is defined by resale transactions, not new construction sales. Buyers actively shopping new construction are not cross-shopping 16-year-old resale at similar prices — these are different buyer pools making different decisions. The PIQ competes in the resale segment and must be priced accordingly.

**WHAT MUST BE VERIFIED**
• Recalculate acquisition basis using resale-only anchor (~$271/sqft)
• Confirm buyer behavior — are buyers choosing new construction over resale at similar price points?
• What would make a buyer choose the PIQ over new construction? (Price discount is the only answer)
• Is the new construction pipeline slowing? Any indication demand may shift to resale inventory?

Do not price 16-year-old resale product as if it were new construction. The market will not accept it.

**CONCLUSION**

The average is contaminated by new construction sales that do not represent the PIQ's competitive position. Resale competes in a fundamentally different market segment at materially lower prices. Anchor to resale comps only and recalculate acquisition basis to reflect achievable value.

# TRAINING NOTES FOR GPT

## Key Behaviors to Reinforce

1. **Always anchor to closed sales** — Closed data is truth. Pendings are directional. Actives are friction.

2. **Call out averages that lie** — Blended averages hide buyer pool splits, outlier contamination, and product type mixing.

3. **Respect visible market rejection** — Stale actives with price reductions define real ceilings, not formula outputs.

4. **Ceiling breach = no closed proof** — If the E-Value exceeds the highest closed, the market hasn't confirmed that value tier.

5. **Pendings are leading indicators** — When pendings consistently differ from closeds, the market is moving.

6. **Scarcity changes the rules** — Low inventory + fast absorption + backup offers = evidence for upper range.

7. **Velocity matters as much as price** — Premium pricing with DOM penalty may underperform baseline pricing with fast turns.

8. **Product type contamination is common** — New construction, different bed/bath counts, and unique features create non-comparable sales.

9. **When signals conflict, verify first** — Mixed signals mean directional bias is unclear. Don't guess — confirm.

10. **Verification tasks must be specific** — "Do more research" is not actionable. "Call agent on 742 Oak pending contract price" is.
